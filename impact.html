Mission & impact

Compliance software shouldn't be a luxury only enterprises can afford.

When small, community-rooted institutions face the same regulatory complexity as Fortune 500 companies, but with a fraction of the resources, they disappear. We're building tools that let independent businesses survive and thrive.

Why this matters

A choice that shouldn't exist

Across the United States, independent businesses in regulated industries face a choice that shouldn't exist. Invest heavily in compliance infrastructure, or sell to a consolidator.

This affects communities most acutely in sectors built on trust and cultural continuity.

Independent funeral homes

Many are minority- and women-owned, and are among the last community institutions many families encounter. But rising regulatory burden (FTC Funeral Rule compliance, state board licensing, pre-need trust management) makes independent operation unsustainable for small firms without large legal teams. When they close, communities lose not just a business, but an institution.

The broader pattern

Regulatory frameworks are written for scale. A compliance burden that represents 2% of a 200-person company's annual spend becomes 15% for a 5-person firm. Compliance becomes a moat for consolidators, not a protection for consumers.

Our approach

Compliance designed for independent operators

Pactura builds AI-powered compliance platforms sized and priced for small teams. We handle the document parsing, rule tracking, and regulatory mapping, and you handle your community.

We're building Pactura's first vertical around independent funeral homes, proving that compliance can work at the business-model level, not just the enterprise level.

  • Turn complex regulations into operational checklists
  • Automate compliance documentation so your team handles the work that requires human judgment
  • Price for revenue, not headcount
  • Build features by talking to the practitioners who use them
Founder connection

Built by someone who knows the systems

I didn't study compliance failures from a distance. I've navigated them.

As a software engineer and founder in Birmingham, Alabama, I've fought credit bureaus over tradeline errors, defended myself in court against debt collection, and pursued a bad-faith insurance claim when systems failed me. Those experiences were messy, frustrating, and instructive. They taught me that opacity in regulated systems falls hardest on people without institutional backing.

That's why I'm building Pactura. Not to solve compliance in the abstract, but to make sure that a small funeral home owner, a minority-owned business, or a family-led firm doesn't have to choose between regulatory survival and staying independent.

I'm also pursuing licensure with the Alabama Board of Funeral Service because funeral directing and grief care are my long-term vocation. This isn't a market I researched. It's a community I'm part of.

The vision

One vertical at a time

Independent funeral homes are where we're starting. But the pattern repeats across regulated industries where small, community-rooted businesses face consolidation pressure.

Each vertical requires domain expertise and real partnerships. We're starting with funeral service because we have it. We'll expand only into sectors where we can build with practitioners, not for them.
